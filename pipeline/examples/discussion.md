# Discussion case: Helix Lab (invented)

**The case.** Helix Lab is a 40-person diagnostics start-up in Barcelona. Two weeks before a funding round, a junior scientist, Laia, tells the COO that a validation study left out 6 of 50 samples "as outliers". With them, accuracy falls from 96% to 89%, below what the pitch deck claims. The CEO says the exclusion follows the protocol. The round closes on Friday.

**Prep questions.** 1. What should the COO do before Friday? 2. Was excluding the samples wrong? 3. What would you change in how Helix Lab runs studies?

## What the pre-notes contain

- The story in eight lines with page references, the decision (what the COO does before Friday) and the cast (Laia, the COO, the CEO, the investors).
- The numbers that matter: 50 samples, 6 excluded, 96% versus 89%, the claim in the deck.
- Terms: "validation study", "outlier", "protocol", in one line each.
- What Claude will ask: "Do you read the protocol as allowing the exclusion? The case gives both readings (p. 3)."

## What the notes contain

- What the session teaches: how to act when the facts are uncertain and the deadline is not.
- Question 1, with evidence on both sides:
  - For delaying the round: the claim in the deck may mislead investors (p. 4); Laia raised it formally (p. 2).
  - For going ahead: the protocol does mention outliers (p. 3); a delay may sink the company (p. 5).
- The course's framework applied, for example stakeholders and their duties.
- Where the case is loose: the protocol's wording is quoted only in part.
- **Your positions to bring to class:** left blank for you.

Nothing here has numbers to check, so there is no workbook.
